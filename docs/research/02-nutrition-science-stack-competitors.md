########## nutrition-science ########## findings: 32

--- SUMMARY ---
The defensible core of menstrual-cycle nutrition is small: iron matters for menstruating women (UK RNI 14.8 mg/day; 25% of women 19–64 are below the LRNI; heavy periods multiply losses 5–6x), the luteal phase raises resting energy expenditure by roughly 30–120 kcal/day and self-reported energy intake by ~170 kcal/day on average (with weighed-intake studies showing smaller or null effects), and core temperature rises ~0.3–0.5 C in the luteal phase. Almost everything else marketed as 'cycle syncing' (phase-specific food lists, seed cycling, 'burn more fat in luteal so cut carbs', training by phase) rests on low-quality or absent evidence: the best meta-analyses find no meaningful phase difference in substrate oxidation (D'Souza 2023) and only a trivial, low-certainty performance dip in the early follicular phase (McNulty 2020). PMS supplement evidence is modest: calcium has the best RCT support (SMD −0.77, high heterogeneity), B6 is positive but from low-quality trials, magnesium is inconsistent. Cycle-phase estimation from period dates is inherently approximate (mean cycle 29.3 d, luteal 12.4 d with a 7–17 d range, only ~13% of cycles are 28 days) and must be switched off for hormonal-contraception users (withdrawal bleeds are not periods) and flagged when variability exceeds 7 days persistently (STRAW+10 perimenopause marker). Menstrual data is special-category health data under UK GDPR Art 9; a purely personal/household app is largely outside UK GDPR scope, but that stops the moment it is offered to others, so design for explicit consent, minimal storage and no third-party trackers anyway. For gym days, consensus position stands (ISSN 2017, ACSM/AND/DC 2016) give protein 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day regardless of training vs rest day, ~0.3–0.4 g/kg per meal across 3–4 meals, and carbohydrate 3–5 g/kg on light days vs 5–7 g/kg on ~1 h/day training days; the practical gym-day delta for a recreational lifter is +200–400 kcal (mostly carbohydrate) and a protein-containing meal within a few hours, not a different diet. For balance scoring, anchor to UK official targets (Eatwell 40/38/12/8/1% by weight over a day or week, 5 x 80 g fruit/veg, 30 g AOAC fibre, free sugars ≤5% energy (~30 g), saturated fat ≤~10% energy (20 g women/30 g men), salt ≤6 g) scored at day/week level, treat '30 plants a week' as a variety heuristic from one cross-sectional citizen-science analysis, and avoid per-food red/green labels, which commercial apps (Noom, Yuka, MyFitnessPal) have been criticised for. Snacks supply ~24% of UK adults' energy (ZOE PREDICT); a 10–15% of TDEE snack allowance counted as food (not 'treats') plus an optional small discretionary allowance mirrors Australian guideline practice (0–2.5 x 600 kJ serves) and UK Eatwell modelling (~3% of energy for HFSS foods). For energy needs use Henry/Oxford equations (SACN 2011) or Mifflin-St Jeor with PAL 1.4/1.6/1.8 and show a ±10% range; Mifflin-St Jeor is within 10% of measured RMR for the largest share of adults (Frankenfield 2005) but individual error remains material.

--- RECOMMENDATION ---
1) Make cycle features opt-in, local-first and conservative. Store only period start dates (and optionally symptoms) with an explicit, separate consent screen naming 'menstrual health data'; encrypt at rest; no third-party analytics on these tables; one-tap delete/export. Phase inference: require ≥3 logged cycles; cycle_length = median of last 6; luteal_estimate = 13 days (Bull 2019 mean 12.4, use 12–14); ovulation_day = cycle_length − 13; phases = menstrual (day 1–5), follicular (6 to ovulation−1), ovulatory (ovulation ±1), luteal (rest); confidence = 'low' when within-person SD >4.5 d or any cycle <21/>35 d; show phase as 'estimated'. Hard-disable phase inference when user selects hormonal contraception (show 'cycle features off: withdrawal bleeds aren't periods'); flag persistent ≥7-day variability with a neutral 'cycles look irregular — phase suggestions paused' (STRAW+10). 2) Cycle 'nudges' (never rules), each tagged with evidence strength in the UI copy: menstrual phase → surface iron-rich recipes (red meat, pulses, fortified cereals, dark greens) paired with vitamin C and note tea/coffee timing (moderate evidence; stronger if user flags heavy periods); luteal → raise the day's snack allowance by ~100 kcal and bias toward satisfying higher-fibre/protein snacks and a calcium-containing option (calcium: moderate PMS evidence) — never '+300 kcal'; no macronutrient shifts by phase (D'Souza null); no seed cycling, no phase food lists; cravings copy is acknowledging, not corrective. 3) Gym-day toggle: protein target unchanged (1.4–2.0 g/kg/day default 1.6; vegan group meal: same target, check leucine-rich sources); per-meal protein target 0.3–0.4 g/kg for 3–4 meals (score breakfast/lunch protein); on gym days add +200–400 kcal (default 300, user-adjustable; or MET x kg x hours) predominantly carbohydrate (move the day from ~3–4 g/kg to ~5–6 g/kg), and require one meal containing ≥0.3 g/kg protein + carbs within ~3 h after the session; rest days: no reduction in protein, carbs float down naturally. 4) Balance score: compute at day and 7-day level (Eatwell explicitly is day/week), never per food. Components (equal-weighted, each 0–1 with soft caps, shown as progress bars not grades): fruit/veg portions (target 5 x 80 g/day; juice counts once, max 150 ml); fibre (30 g/day); protein adequacy (≥1.2 g/kg if active, ≥0.75 g/kg otherwise) and distribution (≥2 meals at ≥0.3 g/kg); saturated fat (≤20 g women/≤30 g men), free sugars (≤30 g), salt (≤6 g) as 'within range' indicators with gentle gradients; Eatwell food-group weight shares vs 40/38/12/8/1 as a weekly radar; variety = distinct plant foods/week displayed as a count with 20–30 as the 'great' band, no fail state. Use UK NPM 2004/05 only internally to tag 'treat-like' recipes for the discretionary allowance, not to colour meals. 5) Snack budget: default snack allowance = 12% of estimated TDEE (≈240 kcal at 2000) counted as food that can earn fibre/5-a-day/protein credit; optional 'treats' allowance = 7% (≈140 kcal) that resets daily and is shown as 'room left' never 'over'; luteal +100 kcal; gym day +100 kcal. Copy is weight-neutral; no 'calories remaining' hero number by default (opt-in). 6) Energy estimate: BMR via Henry/Oxford (weight+age+sex; verify remaining coefficients against Henry 2005 Table 25) with Mifflin-St Jeor as a cross-check when height is given; TDEE = BMR x PAL (1.4/1.6/1.8 picker) + gym-day increment; display as a range (±10%) with a 'this is an estimate; adjust if hungry/full' control; never auto-create deficits; refuse targets below ~1400 kcal without an explicit user override. 7) Keep an evidence registry table in the codebase (claim, strength, source URL, last-reviewed date) and surface a short 'why this suggestion' sheet per nudge so the app stays honest.

- The 2026 Frontiers RMR review has a published correction (20 April 2026) whose content was not seen; its 30–120 kcal/day range should be re-checked against the corrected text.

- Key position stands are ageing: ISSN protein (2017), ISSN nutrient timing (2017), ACSM/AND/DC (2016, erratum 2017), Eatwell Guide (2016), SACN energy (2011), UK NPM (2004/05). None has been superseded as of the sources found, but check for 2025–2026 updates (an ISSN protein stand refresh was rumoured but not found).
- The ISSN 2023 female-athlete stand's luteal protein/energy recommendations are expert-opinion grade and partly conflict with meta-analytic RMR data; the '+300 kcal luteal' figure attributed to it came from a secondary source and was not verified in the full text.
- Snack-energy share (24%) is from the ZOE PREDICT cohort (self-selected app users), not a nationally representative NDNS analysis; no official UK snack-allowance guidance exists, so the 10–15% rule is a design judgement benchmarked on Australian guidelines and Eatwell modelling.
- Commercial app scoring details (ZOE, Lifesum, Noom) are from help-centre pages and third-party reviews; formulas are proprietary and may have changed. Nutri-Score 2023 threshold tables were not retrieved.
- UK GDPR household-scope analysis is general ICO guidance; whether a self-hosted Vercel app used by one household with a cloud database counts as 'purely personal' has not been tested in guidance found. If the app is ever shared beyond the household, full UK GDPR obligations apply.
- Cycle-phase estimation rules (luteal 13 days, variability thresholds) are derived from population data (Natural Cycles, Apple WHS cohorts) that skew toward app users aged 25–45; accuracy for perimenopausal users is poorly validated.

--- OPEN QUESTIONS ---
- Does any household member use hormonal contraception (combined pill/patch/ring, progestogen-only, hormonal IUS) or is perimenopausal? This determines whether cycle-phase features should be built at all for that user profile or shipped as disabled-by-default.
- Does the user want the app to estimate ovulation/phase from dates alone (approximate), or integrate a wearable/BBT source later? The data model should leave room for a 'phase_source' field (calendar vs measured).
- Is the household comfortable with menstrual data in a cloud Postgres on Vercel/Neon (encrypted) or should cycle data be device-local only (IndexedDB) with sync off? This is a privacy-vs-convenience decision only the user can make.
- Weight-neutral default vs explicit calorie targets: should 'calories remaining' and weight goals exist at all, be opt-in, or be excluded? This shapes the whole scoring UI.
- Heavy periods / diagnosed iron deficiency or PMS in the household? If yes, iron-rich and calcium-rich nudges can be weighted higher; if there is any eating-disorder history, snack/energy budgets should be hidden entirely.
- Preferred BMR equation for display: Henry (UK official, no height needed) vs Mifflin-St Jeor (needs height). Requires a live comparison on the household's own data and a decision on whether to collect height/weight at all.
- Default gym-day energy increment: fixed +300 kcal vs session-based (duration x intensity). Needs a few weeks of real use to calibrate; consider a 'how hungry were you?' feedback loop like the prep-time learner.
- Should the weekly balance score be visible to all household members or private per person? Nutrition targets differ by sex/body mass/activity, so per-person scoring is needed but may feel comparative.
- Remaining Henry coefficients and the ACSM 2016 pre-exercise carbohydrate guidance (1–4 g/kg 1–4 h before) should be verified from the primary PDFs on a machine with unrestricted web access before being hard-coded.

The verified picture is still rich: Mealie and Tandoor (the two dominant self-hosted planners) have converged on the same core — per-plan-entry servings that drive shopping quantities, supermarket-ordered shopping lists that learn categories as you drag, constrained random 'fill my week' planners, note/title-only plan entries for leftovers or takeaway, per-user 5-star ratings plus favourites, and a 'last made'/cook-log history — and their 2025-26 issue trackers show exactly where they still hurt: servings drifting between planner and recipe, timezone bugs that shift UK plans by a day in BST, 'add whole day to list' failures, no first-class leftovers (a 2026 community patch had to add it), no multi-recipe meals (open since 2022), and no batch/freezer planning. On rating, no verified mainstream app exposes a two-axis quality × frequency rating: Paprika uses one integer star plus a favourite flag, Mela uses two booleans (favorite, wantToCook), Mealie uses per-user stars + favourite + last_made + timeline 'made' events, Tandoor logs a rating per cook with servings and timestamp; several 2025-26 indie codebases add a 'would make again' boolean or 5-level enum next to stars, which is the closest existing analogue to your Rotation/Time-to-time/If-needs-must/Never-again axis. For motion, primary token sources agree on 150-300 ms for UI transitions: Tailwind v4 defaults to 150 ms with cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1), Material 3 defines short tokens at 50-200 ms and medium at 250-400 ms with an emphasized-decelerate curve, IBM Carbon uses 70/150/400 ms, and the de facto 'premium iOS feel' on the web is Vaul's cubic-bezier(0.32,0.72,0,1). --- RECOMMENDATION ---
1) Data model (highest leverage, all verified pain points): a plan Slot (date as DATE, meal type incl. Snack, person count) holds N Components (recipe, set-breakfast template, pantry item, note, or leftovers-link to an earlier Slot), each with its own portions; quantities on the shopping list are always derived from slot portions, never recipe yield; leftovers components carry zero shopping impact and reserve portions from the source cook (schmidma/meal-prep model); nutrition is stored structured per ingredient (per-100 g + per-portion), never as text. 2) Rating: keep your two explicit axes (5 stars = quality; Rotation / Time to time / If needs must / Never again = desired frequency) but implement them as fields on a per-cook CookLog (Tandoor model) alongside servings, actual prep minutes and a note, with the recipe showing the latest values plus last_made/times_cooked; 'Never again' hides from suggestions, 'If needs must' only surfaces when filters leave nothing else; derive 'due for rotation' from last_made vs frequency tier. No mainstream app does this, so it is a genuine differentiator. 3) Shopping list: Tandoor-style canonical food aliases + one editable Waitrose aisle order + merged quantities with provenance; make 'add week to list' idempotent and diffable (re-running updates, never duplicates — the exact Mealie failure mode). 4) Planner UX: Mealie-style rules + your frequency tier as the 'surprise me' weighting; set breakfasts as reusable component bundles dropped onto slots (answers Tandoor #1337 and Mealie #4527); explicit placeholder entries (takeaway/out/leftovers). 5) Gym days: a manual toggle per day first; optional Hevy webhook/API sync later (requires the user's Hevy Pro key; API is unstable by Hevy's own disclaimer); Strong via CSV only. 6) Cycle phase: store phase as a per-day context tag feeding the same suggestion weighting as gym days; copy must be preference-framed ('warmer, iron-rich, more comforting'), not medical. 7) Motion/aesthetic tokens to adopt: durations 120-150 ms for hover/press/toggle (Tailwind/Carbon moderate-01), 200-250 ms for drag-drop settle and list reorder (Material medium1, ease-out cubic-bezier(0,0,0.2,1) or (0.2,0,0,1)), 300-400 ms only for sheets/drawers using cubic-bezier(0.32,0.72,0,1) (Vaul); honour prefers-reduced-motion; one typeface (Inter variable), 13/15/20/28 px scale, 4 px grid, hairline borders, one accent reserved for state, dark mode generated from the same tokens. 8) Import: schema.org JSON-LD first (recipe-scrapers covers ~640 hosts; port the approach, not the Python), Claude extraction as fallback for pages/photos/video transcripts, and accept Paprika/Mela/Plan to Eat/Mealie/Tandoor archives on day one — but drop imported ratings and free-text nutrition (Norish does the same) and recompute nutrition from ingredients. 9) Explicitly avoid: social feeds, paywalled basics, onboarding quizzes, Apple-only features, and any timestamp-based date handling.

--- OPEN QUESTIONS ---
- Should the frequency rating (Rotation / Time to time / If needs must / Never again) be explicitly set by the user, inferred from the cook log (times cooked, last made, star trend), or both with the explicit value winning? Only you can say how much you want to curate.
- Do you want the planner to be truly multi-user real-time (two people editing the list in-store, as KitchenOwl/Norish/Tandoor do), or is a single shared login with optimistic refresh enough for your household? This decides whether you need Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY or a realtime layer on Vercel.
- Gym days: manual toggle only, or do you (or your partner) hold Hevy Pro and want workouts to flip the toggle automatically via the Hevy API/webhooks? Hevy's API is explicitly 'use at your own risk'.
- Cycle phase: will the phase be entered manually (start-date + typical length) or imported from Flo/Clue/Apple Health? Direct Flo/Clue APIs do not exist publicly; Apple Health export would require a native companion. Also confirm you are comfortable with suggestion-level copy only (no health claims).
- Waitrose aisle order: it must be seeded by hand (or from a first shop) and then learned via drag; is one store layout enough, or do you shop at more than one Waitrose branch/online where order is irrelevant?
- Should imported recipes' ratings and free-text nutrition be discarded (as Norish does) in favour of recomputation, even for a Paprika/Mela library you may already own? Needs a live test with your actual export file.
- Set breakfasts and snacks: do you want them as reusable component bundles (granola + yogurt + berries each with portions) that compute nutrition from parts, or as single 'recipes'? Bundles are more powerful but need a slightly richer UI.
